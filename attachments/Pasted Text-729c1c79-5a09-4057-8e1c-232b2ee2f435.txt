END — S3 #2Analysis and review

This version is substantially stronger because it removes the two drift points entirely: no supernatural knocking and no Malik hearing Elias from another location. Elias finds Malik through workplace knowledge, observation, Mrs. Reed's evidence, and his established physical capability.

That matters because Elias now has a recognizable superhero engine:

A person is endangered → Elias recognizes what others are missing → he intervenes → his abilities create an opening → other people finish the rescue → using those abilities creates exposure risk.

That is sustainable across a season.

The strongest improvement is that his job is part of his competence. “Old docks don't disappear because somebody builds around them” and “I load things that don't want to move” make Elias useful for reasons beyond being strong. He is not a worker pasted onto a superhero. His worker experience affects how he saves people.

The firefighter noticing the bent frame is also the correct kind of superhero consequence. Elias did the right thing, but every public intervention makes anonymity harder. That gives his powers tension without weakening them or inventing artificial limitations.

Malik now serves the season instead of creating a new mystery. His statement—

> “He said I was clocked out. Like that meant I wasn't there.”



—is a direct human-scale version of the Abel/Thomas/Paper conflict. That's exactly how Season 3 should expand: existing mythology becomes real-world stakes, rather than new mythology appearing every issue.

The main reason I wouldn't call it a 10 yet is that the larger Season 3 adversarial pressure is still deliberately offstage. That's correct for Issue #2, but by #3 or #4, someone needs to begin acting against Elias or against people like Malik, not merely systems making mistakes. Otherwise Elias will keep rescuing people from bureaucracy rather than confronting a developing enemy.

But this issue itself is back on course.

The rule I would carry forward is:

> Every SKYBLOOD issue needs something Elias can actually do—not merely something he can discover.



Mystery can deepen around the action. It cannot replace the action.
