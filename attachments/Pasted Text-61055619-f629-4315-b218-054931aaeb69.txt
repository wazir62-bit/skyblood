SKYBLOOD #11 — THE ROOM BEHIND THE WALL

KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.

Inside the house.

Elias and his mother stand perfectly still.

Then again.

KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.

Elias follows the sound.

---

OPEN

Kitchen.

Nothing.

Hallway.

Nothing.

Utility room.

Nothing.

Then—

three knocks.

Elias turns.

They came from inside a wall.

His mother says:

“Leave it.”

Elias looks at her.

“You know what's back there?”

“No.”

“You answered too fast.”

She doesn't argue.

Elias finds where the sound is strongest.

An ordinary wall.

Paint.

Baseboard.

Outlet.

Nothing mystical.

He knocks on it.

Solid.

Moves over.

Knocks again.

Hollow.

His mother:

“Elias.”

He looks at her.

“For ten issues everybody been telling me what not to look at.”

She doesn't understand the reference, of course—but the point is clear.

He's done walking away from questions.

He gets tools.

---

THE OBJECTIVE

The story is simple:

Find out what's inside the wall.

His mother wants him to leave it alone.

Elias wants answers.

And somebody else is about to make sure he doesn't get them.

---

BEHIND THE WALL

Elias carefully opens part of it.

Not a secret temple.

Not glowing hieroglyphics.

Old framing.

Dust.

Insulation.

Then older material behind the newer wall.

The house has been renovated before.

Elias finds a narrow cavity.

Inside:

a wooden box.

Small.

Old.

No magical glow.

No elaborate lock.

His mother sits down when she sees it.

Elias:

“You know this?”

She doesn't answer.

That is an answer.

The box belonged to Elias's grandfather.

His mother remembers seeing it when she was young.

She thought it disappeared after he died.

Elias:

“How it get in my wall?”

“I don't know.”

This time Elias believes her.

He opens it.

---

WHAT GRANDFATHER LEFT

The contents are disappointingly ordinary.

Old work identification.

Receipts.

A pocket notebook.

A key.

Several photographs.

A folded piece of paper.

No spell book.

No superhero costume.

No ancient artifact.

Elias picks up the work ID.

His grandfather.

Same employer lineage connected to the old assignment from #10.

Different era.

Different company name.

Same industrial network.

Now Elias has physical proof.

He opens the notebook.

Most of it is mundane:

hours.

loads.

weather.

names.

money owed.

appointments.

Then Elias notices something.

Weather.

His grandfather recorded weather.

Not poetically.

Not scientifically.

Short entries.

Pressure early.

Wind wrong.

Clouds holding.

Waited.

Elias stops.

Those aren't his words.

But they feel like his thoughts.

His mother sees his face.

“What?”

Elias closes the notebook.

“Nothing.”

He's not ready to tell her.

---

THE KNOCKING

Then the wall knocks again.

Three times.

Elias and his mother stare at the open cavity.

Nothing inside could have made the sound.

Elias reaches in.

His hand touches the back surface.

Cold.

Then he realizes the sound isn't coming from this wall anymore.

KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.

Front door.

Someone is outside.

---

THE WOMAN AT THE DOOR

Elias looks through the window first.

A woman stands on the porch.

Mid-40s to 50s.

Professional.

Umbrella.

Ordinary car.

Nothing theatrical.

Elias opens the door but doesn't invite her inside.

She identifies herself.

Dr. Mara Voss.

Historical preservation consultant.

She says she's researching old industrial properties in Memphis.

Then she says the address from #10.

Elias doesn't react.

Voss:

“I believe you recently asked about it.”

Now Elias reacts.

“How you know that?”

“People remember a six-foot-four man asking unusual questions.”

Reasonable answer.

Almost funny.

She asks whether Elias found anything associated with the old property.

“No.”

Another lie.

Voss looks past him.

His mother is standing inside.

Voss's expression changes.

Recognition.

Mother:

“Do I know you?”

Voss:

“No, ma'am.”

Again—

too fast.

---

THE HUMAN THREAT

Voss isn't threatening.

That's what makes her effective.

She explains that the old property has attracted rumors for decades.

Accidents.

Bad records.

Stories.

People turn ordinary coincidences into mythology.

She has spent years separating history from nonsense.

Elias:

“Then why you at my house?”

Good question.

Voss says an archival request containing his name crossed her desk.

She wanted to make sure he didn't disturb anything dangerous at the old property.

Elias:

“Dangerous how?”

“Structurally.”

Perfectly rational.

Then she sees something behind Elias.

Grandfather's notebook.

Only a corner.

Her composure changes for half a second.

Elias notices.

Now he knows:

She came for something.

---

THE CHASE WITHOUT A CHASE

Elias doesn't confront her immediately.

He thanks her.

Closes the door.

Locks it.

His mother:

“She lying.”

Elias:

“I know.”

Mother:

“How?”

Elias:

“She looked at the book.”

Mother:

“What book?”

Elias realizes something.

His mother hadn't seen him take the notebook from the box.

He turns.

The notebook isn't where he left it.

Gone.

Elias opens the door.

Voss is already walking toward her car.

“HEY.”

She turns.

Elias approaches.

“You take something out my house?”

Voss:

“No.”

“Open your bag.”

“No.”

Not villainous.

Firm.

Elias steps closer.

She doesn't retreat.

Then:

“If you think I stole something, call the police.”

Elias has no proof.

And she knows it.

Voss gets into her car.

Leaves.

Elias watches.

He doesn't chase her.

Because he notices something through the front window.

The notebook.

Still inside.

On the floor.

It had fallen behind a chair.

Elias looks back toward Voss's departing car.

He was wrong.

That matters.

---

ELIAS LEARNS SOMETHING

Back inside, his mother tells him:

“You looking so hard for something, everything start looking like it.”

That's the line of the issue.

It hits Elias.

The mythology cannot turn him stupid.

Suspicion cannot become evidence.

If Elias is going to understand what's happening, he has to learn the difference between:

what he knows,

what he senses,

and

what he fears.

That's part of becoming SKYBLOOD too.

He apologizes to his mother.

Then picks up the notebook.

This time he reads carefully.

---

THE DISCOVERY

Most entries remain mundane.

Then a sequence appears repeatedly.

Not every storm.

Only certain ones.

Grandfather would write:

HEARD 3.

Sometimes:

DIDN'T ANSWER.

Once:

ANSWERED. NOTHING.

Then years later:

HEARD 3. ELIAS SAFE.

Elias stops.

He wasn't born yet.

He checks the date.

Years before his birth.

There it is again.

His name.

But we learned our lesson from the discarded version of #10:

We do not pile another giant mystery onto this.

Elias notices something below it.

Different handwriting.

Smaller.

Probably added later.

NOT A NAME.

That changes the question.

Maybe “Elias” wasn't referring to him.

Now the reader has something understandable to hold.

Elias:

“What else does Elias mean?”

His mother doesn't know.

---

THE KEY

There is still a key in the box.

Elias examines it.

A number stamped into it.

No symbol.

No mystical decoration.

His mother recognizes the type.

Old locker key.

Grandfather used to carry one for work.

Elias remembers the old dead address.

The employer lineage.

The industrial property.

This is actionable.

He doesn't need to decipher mythology.

He needs to find the locker.

---

THE CLIMAX

Before Elias can leave, thunder.

The approaching storm from #10 hasn't fully passed.

Lights flicker.

Then:

KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.

Not the wall.

Not the front door.

The wooden box.

Both hear it.

Elias slowly approaches.

Another three.

From inside.

Impossible.

He opens it.

Nothing.

He removes everything.

Empty.

Another:

KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.

But now Elias realizes something.

It's vibration.

Not knocking.

The box is sitting over a floor vent.

Something below the house is causing the ductwork to strike rhythmically.

Grounded explanation.

Elias follows it.

HVAC.

Pipe.

Pressure.

Storm runoff.

He finds the source.

A loose section of old ductwork.

Every pressure change makes it hit the framing three times.

His mother almost laughs.

“So all this—”

Elias:

“No.”

Because the wall knocked before the weather changed.

And his grandfather wrote about three knocks decades ago.

But Elias has learned something important:

Not every mystery is supernatural just because it resembles one.

He fixes the duct.

The knocking stops.

Silence.

Then his phone rings.

W.

Elias answers.

W:

“You at home?”

“Yeah.”

W is quiet.

Then:

“Somebody just knocked on my door three times.”

Elias's expression changes.

“Don't open it.”

W:

“Why?”

Elias grabs the grandfather's notebook and key.

“Because I'm coming.”

---

LAST PAGE

Before Elias leaves, we see the open notebook.

Elias doesn't notice what the reader can.

Several weather entries contain tiny marks in the margins.

Meaningless scratches at first glance.

Across multiple pages, if mentally connected, they reproduce part of the geometry we've been hiding since earlier issues.

Beside one entry is a tiny hand-drawn shape:

a doorway beneath three points.

No explanation.

Then we cut to W's house.

W waits.

Someone knocks again.

KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.

W looks through the peephole.

We do not show what he sees.

We show W.

Confusion.

Then recognition.

W whispers:

“Man... what the hell you doing here?”

END.

ISSUE #11 ENGINE

WANT: Elias wants to discover what was knocking inside his house.

OBSTACLE: His mother is afraid of the past, Elias doesn't know which clues are real, and an outsider appears asking about the same history.

STAKES: The mystery connected to W and Elias's grandfather has reached Elias's home.

DISCOVERY: Grandfather experienced unusual weather awareness and documented the three knocks.

ACTION: Elias stops merely reacting and begins separating evidence from assumption.

CLIMAX: Elias discovers one apparent supernatural knocking has an ordinary explanation.

That's important.

CONSEQUENCE: Immediately afterward, W experiences the same three-knock pattern at his own door.

TRANSFORMATION: Elias learns a fundamental rule:

Sensing something doesn't mean understanding it.

That principle will eventually matter enormously when his abilities become stronger.

ONE FOREGROUND SUPERNATURAL QUESTION:

What do the three knocks actually signify?

Everything serves that question.

---

DR. MARA VOSS

Voss is our first potential recurring human pressure point.

But we do not establish her as the villain.

She may know something.

She may be protecting something.

She may genuinely be a historian.

She may have lied.

She may have told Elias considerably more truth than he realizes.

Most importantly:

Elias falsely accuses her once.

That protects SKYBLOOD from becoming “mysterious person = evil.”

When she eventually does something suspicious, readers won't automatically know whether Elias should trust his instincts.

---

THE HIDDEN SKYBLOOD LAYER

THREE KNOCKS

On the surface: recurring mystery.

Underneath, three can connect eventually to multiple traditions without us declaring which interpretation is correct.

Three stages.

Three points.

Three gates.

Three positions.

Past / present / future.

Birth / life / death.

Body / mind / something else.

We choose the actual SKYBLOOD meaning later based on the deeper mythology—not because one internet occult interpretation sounds cool.

MASONIC / ARCHITECTURAL LAYER

The house compositions quietly use paired vertical structures.

Doorframes.

Studs exposed inside the wall.

Porch supports.

Shelving.

Industrial framing.

They visually echo pillars and thresholds without characters discussing Masonry.

At several points Elias occupies the space between two supports.

That position will eventually matter.

EGYPTIAN LAYER

The hidden visual theme isn't pyramids or hieroglyphs.

It's passage.

Doors.

Boundaries.

Crossing from one state/place into another.

The mythology remains conceptual before it becomes iconographic.

ILLUMINATED IMAGERY

When Elias opens his grandfather's box, the overhead composition should resemble an illuminated manuscript page without announcing itself.

Box = central panel.

Tools, wood fragments and household objects = marginal decoration.

Elias's hands enter the composition from below.

Ordinary objects arranged with sacred visual gravity.

---

THE EASTER EGG

Dr. Voss carries an umbrella.

Completely ordinary.

On reread, its internal ribs form geometry we've seen elsewhere.

We never center it.

We never mention it.

Years later, readers may argue whether Voss deliberately chose it.

We may never answer.

---

THE GEM

Grandfather's notebook contains dozens of mundane numbers.

Hours.

Weights.

Dates.

Loads.

Most are meaningless.

But several entries associated with HEARD 3 contain a numerical relationship.

Nobody notices.

Much later, when SKYBLOOD's astronomical layer becomes clearer, readers can return to #11 and discover:

Grandfather wasn't recording when the knocking occurred.

Without understanding it—

he was recording when it was possible.

---

WHAT #11 DOES NOT ANSWER

We don't explain the three knocks.

We don't explain what “ELIAS” meant in grandfather's notebook.

We don't identify Voss as friend or enemy.

We don't reveal what W sees.

We don't give Elias another power.

We don't identify a secret society.

We don't say Freemasonry caused anything.

We don't say Egyptian mythology caused anything.

We don't explain the geometry.

We don't open five new mysteries.

We take the mystery we've already earned—

three knocks—

and make it matter.

First read:

“Elias found his grandfather's notebook, and now whatever happened to his grandfather has reached W.”

Second read:

“Some of those knocks weren't supernatural.”

Later reread:

“His grandfather was recording windows of occurrence before anybody understood what they were.”

That's where SKYBLOOD should live:

Clear enough to follow. Deep enough to study.
