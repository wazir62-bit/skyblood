SKYBLOOD #9 — THE WEIGHT OF HEAVEN

The storm is gone.

But something didn't leave with it.

After the real klaxon event, Elias returns to work expecting questions about what happened.

Instead, almost nobody wants to talk about it.

People remember the emergency.

They remember running.

They remember Elias going the wrong direction.

But when Elias asks about one particular moment on the field, the answers don't match.

Three people saw three different things.

One remembers Elias disappearing behind equipment.

Another swears he never lost sight of him.

A third remembers looking directly at Elias—

and seeing the sky behind him.

Elias lets it go.

At least outwardly.

Then something follows him home.

THE THREAT

It begins with weight.

A glass tips over without falling.

A hanging light leans several degrees toward Elias.

Rainwater trapped in a gutter runs briefly uphill.

At work, a loose strap hangs toward him instead of toward the ground.

Nothing spectacular.

Nothing Elias can demonstrate on command.

And whenever he tries to reproduce it, nothing happens.

Then his mother notices something.

Not the phenomenon.

Elias.

She asks why he's standing like that.

He doesn't understand.

She tells him:

“You been walking like something heavy sitting on you.”

Elias dismisses it.

But that night, he wakes standing in his bedroom.

Barefoot.

Facing east.

His feet hurt.

There is dirt underneath them.

He has no memory of leaving the house.

THE DISCOVERY

The next day Elias retraces what he can.

There are muddy impressions outside.

His.

They lead away from the house.

But they don't lead back.

He follows them.

Across the property.

Toward the street.

Then they stop at the pavement.

Across the road is an old building Elias has passed hundreds of times.

Nothing mystical.

Nothing abandoned.

Nothing announcing itself as important.

But today something about its entrance bothers him.

The proportions are wrong.

Or maybe too right.

He counts without intending to.

Steps.

Columns.

Window divisions.

He stops himself.

Elias has never cared about architecture.

So why does he already know how many steps are there before he finishes counting them?

He crosses the street.

THE ACTION

Inside, the ordinary world remains ordinary.

People work.

Phones ring.

Someone complains about the weather.

Elias almost feels stupid for coming.

Then he sees a mark.

Small.

Old.

Partially covered by decades of paint.

It resembles nothing the average person would recognize as important.

Elias touches it.

Nothing happens.

Good.

He laughs at himself.

Then someone behind him says:

“You shouldn't have done that.”

Elias turns.

An older man is watching him.

Not frightened.

Not impressed.

Angry.

Elias asks what the symbol means.

The man refuses to answer.

Elias asks why he shouldn't touch it.

The man's answer is stranger:

“Because it didn't belong there until you did.”

Elias looks back.

The mark is still there.

Same chipped paint.

Same age.

Impossible.

The older man walks away.

Elias follows.

By the time Elias reaches the next corridor—

the man is gone.

There is nowhere obvious he could have gone.

But this is not the real danger.

Outside, something has changed.

THE CLIMAX

The weight returns.

Hard.

Elias drops to one knee.

People continue walking.

Nobody reacts.

He tries standing.

Can't.

His palms hit the pavement.

The pressure isn't crushing the city.

It is crushing him.

For the first time, Elias understands something terrifying:

The sky isn't always responding to him.

Sometimes—

something on the other side may be pulling.

He looks upward.

Clear sky.

Then he notices a man stepping into the street.

Traffic coming.

The man doesn't see it.

Elias can't stand.

Can't summon wind.

Can't control anything.

He shouts.

Not enough.

The vehicle keeps coming.

Elias forces himself upright against whatever is holding him.

One step.

Then another.

Every movement looks physically impossible from Elias's perspective—

but perfectly ordinary to everyone else.

He reaches the man.

Shoves him clear.

The vehicle misses them.

And immediately—

the weight disappears.

Elias lies in the street.

The rescued man is furious, confused, frightened.

He says there wasn't a car.

Elias turns.

The road is empty.

No brakes.

No vehicle disappearing into the distance.

Nothing.

Elias looks toward the old building.

The older man is standing at an upstairs window.

Watching.

Then someone passes between them.

The window is empty.

THE LAST PAGE

That night Elias draws the mark from memory.

He doesn't know why.

He gets it wrong.

Tears out the page.

Draws it again.

Wrong.

Again.

Wrong.

Finally Elias stops trying to remember the shape.

He remembers how the building made him feel.

His hand moves.

This time the drawing is different from the mark he saw.

Elias stares at it.

We do not identify it.

We do not translate it.

We do not explain it.

Then Elias notices something else.

The dirt beneath his fingernails from the night before isn't brown.

Under the lamp—

there are tiny blue-black particles inside it.

Elias looks toward his bedroom window.

Outside:

clear sky.

One star appears unusually bright.

Then another.

Then another.

They form no constellation Elias recognizes.

But on Elias's desk, without the narration calling attention to it, the three points correspond to three positions in the symbol he just drew.

END.

ISSUE ENGINE

MYSTERY: Why did Elias apparently leave his house without remembering it?

THREAT: An invisible force begins exerting physical influence specifically around Elias.

REACTION: Elias investigates instead of waiting for another phenomenon.

DISCOVERY: Something hidden inside an ordinary piece of his city appears connected to what is happening.

ACTION: Elias crosses a boundary voluntarily for the first time.

CLIMAX: The mysterious force physically restrains Elias while apparent danger forces him to choose whether to act anyway.

CONSEQUENCE: Elias saves someone—but cannot prove that the danger he saved him from ever existed.

TRANSFORMATION: In #8 Elias walked toward danger without W. In #9 Elias does something new: he walks toward the mystery.

DEEPER MYSTERY: Is Elias perceiving things other people cannot see—or is something learning how to control what Elias perceives?

THE HIDDEN ISSUE

The title THE WEIGHT OF HEAVEN works literally, psychologically and mythologically.

The building quietly carries architectural ratios and alignments that can support later Masonic, Egyptian and Greek interpretations without explicitly identifying any tradition here.

Doorways become a recurring visual language.

Elias crosses thresholds repeatedly throughout the issue without narration explaining their importance.

Vertical lines dominate before the weight begins.

Horizontal lines dominate while Elias is pinned.

The visual composition changes again when he stands.

The older man's position should repeatedly place him where an illuminated manuscript might place a marginal figure: present at the edge of the story, apparently secondary, but watching the central action.

Three points recur.

Not enough to explain themselves.

The number should appear naturally—in architecture, blocking and background detail—never with a character saying, “Three again.”

And somewhere in the building should be one tiny visual element already seen in an earlier SKYBLOOD issue.

We do not identify it.

We don't zoom in.

We don't explain it.

Years later, that panel may become evidence.

WHAT #9 MUST NOT ANSWER

We do not establish that the older man is supernatural.

We do not establish that the building belongs to a secret society.

We do not establish that the mark is Egyptian, Greek, Masonic or anything else.

We do not establish whether Elias actually sleepwalked.

We do not establish whether the vehicle existed.

We do not give Elias gravity powers.

We do not explain the stars.

And we absolutely do not tell the reader what the symbol means.

Because after #9 the audience should begin asking a more dangerous question than:

“What powers does Elias have?”

They should start asking:

“What can see Elias now that Elias has started seeing it?”
