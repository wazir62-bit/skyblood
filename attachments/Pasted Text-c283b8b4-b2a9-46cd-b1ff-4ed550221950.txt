END — S3 #4Canon analysis

This stays in the superhero lane while making the season smarter.

The key distinction is T-17 versus B-6. T-17 is deliberate sabotage. B-6 is ordinary negligence made dangerous because its status no longer matches its physical condition. That prevents us from turning every accident into the work of a mastermind.

More importantly, Elias actually investigates through his worker knowledge. Missing service-tag sequence → B-6 → defective hose → he realizes people are already using it. His intelligence and occupational experience cause the rescue. His powers finish it.

The action also follows SB-CB01 exactly. The memorable image is Elias catching a falling worker by an ordinary lime safety vest. Lime means see this worker so he doesn't die. It doesn't transform into Elias's costume. There is no recognition-gold, no aura, and the bent rail provides the physical evidence of his strength.

There's also superhero cost. Elias's previous injury matters, his leg worsens, another worker witnesses impossible strength, and ultimately his own employer removes him from ramp duty because he keeps getting hurt. That creates a useful problem: we have temporarily separated Elias from the workplace where much of his superhero competence and identity lives.

The ending also advances the season's central argument without introducing mythology:

> Somebody cut one line. Nobody had to cut the other.



Elias is beginning to understand that deliberate evil and institutional failure can produce the same endangered body.
