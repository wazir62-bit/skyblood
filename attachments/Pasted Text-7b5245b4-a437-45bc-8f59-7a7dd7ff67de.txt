Yes. A is the right spine, but I would sharpen one thing: don't make #8 merely an episode about W getting moved. Make it the first time Elias learns that whatever is happening around him can hurt other people's lives without physically touching them.

What I would strengthen

1. Make W's removal feel reasonable.
The people taking W off nights shouldn't behave like villains. From their perspective, they are reducing risk after Elias's early timestamps and the unresolved crew problem. That makes the system frightening because nobody has to be evil.

2. Don't announce the consequence immediately.
Let Elias arrive expecting W. His absence should reveal itself naturally: somebody else standing where W stands, an unanswered remark, a different rhythm in the crew. Then Elias learns W has been reassigned. The reader experiences the missing person before receiving the explanation.

3. Give W one small scene before he's removed.
Nothing sentimental. Something ordinary between W and Elias. That gives the absence weight later without telegraphing, W is about to disappear.

4. Make Elias partly responsible—but ambiguously.
His timestamps were truthful. His sensing may even have protected people. Yet those same records become justification for separating W from the shift.

That's excellent SKYBLOOD territory:

> What if doing the right thing produces the wrong consequence?



5. The real danger should prove Elias wasn't imagining things.
When the klaxon finally becomes real, circumstances should resemble something Elias sensed earlier closely enough that the audience realizes: he was right.

But being right doesn't save him.

That's crucial.

6. Don't let Elias compensate with a new ability.
No convenient gust. No secret rescue power. No sudden upgrade because the climax arrived.

The emerging superhero has to solve the immediate problem with Elias Marrow's judgment, courage, body, and whatever he has learned so far.

That makes later powers mean more.

7. Make W's absence alter Elias's behavior.
This is where your superhero-birth doctrine enters #8. Elias has previously had someone who walked toward him. Now that person isn't there.

The climax forces a choice:

Does Elias still move toward danger when nobody is moving toward him?

That's superhero construction without putting him in a cape.

One mystery seed I'd add

After everything is over, Elias discovers one tiny inconsistency concerning W's reassignment.

Not proof of conspiracy.

Not Egyptian imagery.

Not Anubis.

Not the crew-photo name.

Just something that makes Elias—and the reader—wonder:

Was W moved because of Elias's timestamps... or were the timestamps merely the available excuse to move W?

Don't answer it in #8.

That single question gives The Other Road two stories simultaneously:

Surface story: workplace safety consequences separate two men.

Hidden story: somebody may already understand that certain people need to be separated from Elias.

And if we later reveal there was nothing conspiratorial about W's reassignment, that's still useful—the audience will have begun learning that in SKYBLOOD, even they can become paranoid trying to distinguish coincidence from design.

That feels very much like the kind of mystery engine you said you want for this series.
