TO BE CONTINUED.Revised review: 9.9/10

This is the strongest version of Issue #10.

The biggest improvement is Abel's final conclusion. Instead of the script declaring:

> Thomas kept himself.



we now get:

> “I think Thomas found a way to stay.”



That's a hypothesis from a traumatized participant with damaged memory—not canon exposition.

Even better, Abel understands the danger of allowing the voice inside 2417 to fill his missing memory:

> “Don't let that thing tell you for me.”



That's character growth. Earlier Abel was terrified of what he couldn't remember. Now he recognizes that an answer supplied by an interested party may be worse than uncertainty.

Paper is fixed — 10/10

Changing:

> REOPEN?



to:

> REOPENING ELIGIBLE



restores Paper's grammar.

Paper doesn't ask.

Paper classifies, updates, files, reviews, closes and determines eligibility.

That distinction matters because the systems are becoming increasingly complex. Their voices must remain unmistakable.

“Everybody after this” is much better

This preserves chronology as a possibility without announcing time travel.

And Mae immediately asks:

> After what?



Abel doesn't answer.

Perfect.

The reader now knows “this” is an event boundary, but not whether its consequences alter memory, records, causality, witnessing, or something else.

Abel's name is now fully paid

This mystery is finished.

Mae independently calls him Abel.

Thomas independently calls him Abel.

The tape predates the present investigation.

Abel recognizes the evidence.

And finally:

> ABEL: Yeah.



That's his acceptance.

From this issue forward, he is Abel. No qualifier needed.

That is exactly the kind of payoff a mystery-heavy serial needs: an established promise gets a definitive landing while larger questions remain open. 

The ending is better because nothing explodes

I especially like ending with:

> silence.



After several issues of voices, gold, Paper updates and cliffhanger declarations, denying the darkness the final line gives Abel a small but meaningful victory.

It's not power.

It's epistemic agency:

You don't get to tell me what I remember.

That's deeply consistent with what SKYBLOOD has become.
