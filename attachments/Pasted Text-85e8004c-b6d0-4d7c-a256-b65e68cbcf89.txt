SKYBLOOD #10 — THE ADDRESS

W didn't choose that road.

Somebody gave it to him.

---

OPEN

W is home recovering.

Bruised leg. Crutches. Irritated that everybody keeps asking if he needs anything.

Elias visits.

Their friendship has changed.

Not dramatically.

W still jokes.

Elias still gives it back.

But now they share something neither man can explain.

W finally asks:

“You hear them knocks again?”

“No.”

“Good.”

Pause.

“You?”

“No.”

Neither sounds convinced.

W has been trying to reconstruct the day he disappeared.

Gas station.

Directions.

Road.

Something familiar.

Then the fall.

He remembers one new thing.

Before the gas station, somebody gave him an address.

Elias:

“Who?”

W:

“That's the problem.”

He doesn't remember.

But he remembers where the address came from.

Work.

---

THE OBJECTIVE

The surface story is simple:

Elias wants to find out who sent W to that address.

That's Issue #10.

No second quest.

No new artifact hunt.

No mythology lesson.

Elias returns to work.

---

FOLLOW THE PAPER

Elias starts with the reassignment.

W was officially moved from nights after the safety concerns surrounding Elias's weather timestamps.

That part is legitimate.

There is a supervisor.

There is a decision.

There is a record.

But the record says W was assigned to a day crew at another operating location for orientation.

There's the address.

Elias recognizes the road immediately.

It's where W disappeared.

Now #9 changes.

W wasn't wandering randomly.

He was following instructions.

Elias asks who entered the destination.

The supervisor doesn't know.

Computer-generated assignment.

Routine.

Elias asks:

“Generated from what?”

A list.

“What list?”

Available work locations.

Simple.

Boring.

Until Elias asks whether the location is still active.

The supervisor checks.

“No.”

“How long?”

Another check.

The location closed years ago.

Silence.

The supervisor looks genuinely disturbed.

Not sinister.

Not part of anything.

He says:

“That shouldn't be in there.”

Now Elias has something real.

---

ELIAS MAKES A CHOICE

Management wants to treat it as a database error.

And maybe it is.

Old location remained in an old table.

W got routed there accidentally.

Bad data.

Bad luck.

Case closed.

That explanation is plausible.

Elias almost accepts it.

Then he asks:

“Who else got sent there?”

Nobody knows.

That's the question that drives the rest of the issue.

---

THE LIST

Elias finds a way to see older assignments.

Nothing illegal or hacker-like.

He asks.

Pushes.

Waits.

Uses people who know where records are.

This is Elias learning another kind of heroism:

Sometimes you don't punch through a wall.

Sometimes you refuse to stop asking a reasonable question.

They find several old entries connected to the dead address.

Not hundreds.

Not an obvious conspiracy.

A handful.

Spread across years.

Different employees.

Different departments.

Different circumstances.

Most were corrected before anyone went there.

One wasn't.

W.

Then Elias finds another.

Much older.

The employee name is missing from the copy.

But the destination is the same.

Elias asks what happened.

The older worker helping him doesn't know.

Then:

“Wait.”

He remembers hearing about somebody getting hurt near there years ago.

That's enough.

Elias goes back.

---

RETURN TO THE ROAD

This time Elias knows exactly why he's there.

He's not chasing a vision.

He's investigating an address.

Daylight.

Traffic.

Ordinary city noise.

The service structure where W was trapped is fenced off after the collapse.

Elias can't enter.

Good.

We don't repeat #9.

Instead, Elias walks the perimeter.

He compares the address with the surrounding buildings.

Something doesn't make sense.

The address technically belongs to the old property—

but the location where W was found isn't the entrance.

It is behind it.

Elias follows the block.

Around the other side he finds what used to be the front.

Mostly rebuilt.

New businesses.

Old masonry incorporated into newer construction.

And above a sealed doorway:

an old number.

The same address.

Elias photographs it.

---

THE MAN

Someone sees him.

An older man comes out of a nearby business.

Not mystical.

Not ominous.

He asks whether Elias needs help.

Elias asks what used to be there.

The man remembers.

Warehouse.

Before that, something else.

He isn't sure.

Elias shows him W's photograph from #9.

“Recognize this?”

The man studies it.

He recognizes the wall.

Then he sees the distant figure.

His expression changes.

Elias catches it.

“You know him?”

“No.”

“You looked like you did.”

“I know where he's standing.”

That's better.

The man points.

Across the road.

Empty sidewalk.

“Right there.”

Elias:

“So?”

The man:

“Nothing.”

He starts walking away.

Elias follows.

“What was there?”

The man stops.

“A door.”

Elias looks.

There is no door.

Not even a building.

Just roadway and newer construction.

Elias:

“To what?”

The man answers:

“I don't know.”

And keeps walking.

That's all he gets.

We do not turn the old man into a mythology professor.

---

THE THREAT

Elias examines historical property records at a public archive/library.

Again: grounded investigation.

Old maps.

Photographs.

Addresses.

City changes.

He finds the property.

There was indeed a structure where the man indicated.

So the man wasn't lying.

Elias works backward.

Different decades.

Different building configurations.

But something strange emerges.

The street changed.

Buildings changed.

Parcel lines changed.

Yet the same address appears repeatedly.

Sometimes attached to different physical structures.

That's weird.

But still potentially explainable through renumbering and municipal records.

Then Elias finds an old photograph.

Workers standing outside one version of the property.

Ordinary men.

Work clothes.

Nothing supernatural.

Elias almost moves on.

Then he sees someone he recognizes.

Not himself.

Not W.

His grandfather.

Now this is personal.

---

THE FAMILY CONNECTION

Elias takes the photograph home.

His mother recognizes her father immediately.

Elias asks:

“He worked there?”

She didn't know.

Maybe temporary work.

People worked places.

Elias points to the address.

Her reaction is tiny.

But Elias sees it.

“You know that number?”

“No.”

“Ma.”

“I said no.”

Elias doesn't push immediately.

Then she says:

“Your grandfather didn't like storms.”

Elias waits.

“He'd turn everything off.”

“What everything?”

“TV. Radio. Lights sometimes.”

“Why?”

She shrugs.

“He was old.”

Elias:

“He wasn't old when you were a kid.”

That gets him a look.

Then she remembers something.

During bad storms, her father would stand near the front door.

Not looking outside.

Listening.

Elias asks:

“For what?”

Her answer:

“Knocking.”

Now #9 pays off.

Three knocks.

Pause.

Three knocks.

Elias doesn't tell her what he heard.

He asks:

“What happened if he heard it?”

Mother:

“He never opened the door.”

---

THE CLIMAX

That night a storm approaches Memphis.

Nothing apocalyptic.

Real thunderstorm.

Elias is home.

Mother asleep.

He sits with the old photograph and W's picture.

He has enough information to understand that something about that address touched his family before it touched W.

But he doesn't know what.

Thunder.

Rain.

Then:

KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.

Elias freezes.

Front door.

Silence.

Then again.

KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.

Not supernatural thunder.

Someone is actually at the door.

His mother's bedroom light comes on.

She walks into the hallway.

Sees Elias.

Then hears it.

Her face changes.

For the first time, Elias knows this mystery isn't only happening inside his head.

His mother whispers:

“Don't.”

Elias:

“Don't what?”

Another three knocks.

She says:

“Don't open that door.”

Now Elias has a choice.

His grandfather refused to open it.

His mother wants the same thing.

But Elias has spent ten issues having things happen to him.

This time he chooses.

He approaches the door.

Mother:

“Elias.”

He puts his hand on the lock.

Opens it.

---

WHAT'S OUTSIDE

A man is standing in the rain.

Completely ordinary.

Delivery jacket.

Wet.

Annoyed.

Holding an envelope.

“Elias Marrow?”

Elias almost laughs from relief.

“Yeah.”

“Need a signature.”

Elias takes the envelope.

The man leaves.

Mother is furious.

“You don't open a door because somebody knocks?”

Elias:

“That's generally how doors work.”

She doesn't laugh.

Elias looks at the envelope.

No return address.

His name.

Correct address.

Inside:

one sheet of paper.

No ancient writing.

No symbol.

No prophecy.

A photocopy of an old employee assignment.

The dead work address.

A date decades earlier.

And the employee name:

Elias's grandfather.

At the bottom is a handwritten notation:

DID NOT REPORT.

Elias looks toward his mother.

She has gone pale.

Then—

from somewhere inside the house:

KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.

Elias turns.

Mother turns.

Not the front door.

The sound came from farther inside.

Three knocks.

Pause.

Three knocks.

END.

---

ISSUE #10 ENGINE

WANT: Elias wants to know who sent W to the dead address.

OBSTACLE: The assignment appears to have been generated from ordinary company records, and nobody knows why a closed location remained available.

STAKES: If this wasn't random, somebody—or something—may have deliberately sent W there.

The surface story remains understandable from beginning to end.

THREAT: The address that nearly killed W has a history.

REACTION: Elias investigates.

DISCOVERY: The same address appears across years and is connected to Elias's grandfather.

ACTION: Elias follows the paper trail instead of waiting for another phenomenon.

CLIMAX: The three knocks reach Elias's own home, and he deliberately does what his grandfather apparently would not:

He opens the door.

CONSEQUENCE: Elias receives proof that his grandfather was once assigned to the same location as W.

TRANSFORMATION: Elias stops asking only, “Why is this happening to me?”

Now he has evidence to ask:

“How long has this been happening to my family?”

ONE FOREGROUND SUPERNATURAL QUESTION:

Who—or what—is knocking?

Nothing else competes with it.

---

THE HIDDEN SKYBLOOD LAYER

The surface plot is paperwork → address → history → family → knocking.

Simple.

Underneath it, we bury the treasure.

THE ADDRESS

The digits should be selected deliberately and then never explained.

Their sum, spacing or relationship can later connect to SKYBLOOD's numerical mythology.

Readers who notice early get a piece.

Readers who don't lose nothing.

THE DOOR

Doors quietly dominate #10.

Loading doors.

Vehicle doors.

Archive doors.

Automatic doors.

Old sealed entrances.

Elias's front door.

We never have narration say:

“Doors keep appearing.”

That's for rereaders.

The Masonic/esoteric layer can use thresholds, orientation, paired columns and measured spaces without showing an obvious square-and-compasses emblem.

Egyptian echoes can exist through ideas of passage, judgment and guarded thresholds.

Greek echoes can operate through crossroads, routes and boundaries.

None is identified as the explanation.

Different traditions may simply preserve pieces of something older.

ILLUMINATED IMAGERY

One archive panel should quietly resemble an illuminated manuscript page.

Shelving and architecture create the border.

A hanging lamp forms the upper ornament.

Elias occupies the position traditionally reserved for the central figure.

But he's reading mundane municipal records.

No glowing book.

No mystical spectacle.

The sacred-looking composition surrounds something completely ordinary.

That's SKYBLOOD.

THREE

Three knocks become our strongest audible motif so far.

But don't make every three meaningful.

There should be meaningless groups of three too.

Otherwise readers learn the code too easily.

---

THE EASTER EGG

In the old employee photograph containing Elias's grandfather, there are several workers.

One stands partially behind another.

Almost invisible.

Later seasons can reveal that person matters.

But here's the trick:

The person is not looking at the camera.

They're looking upward.

Readers coming back years later will realize this mirrors W at the gas station in #9.

Two men.

Decades apart.

Same address.

Same behavior.

We don't mention it.

---

THE GEM

On the old map Elias examines, several streets intersect around the dead address.

Viewed normally:

city streets.

Viewed upside down:

still city streets.

But rotated to the orientation Elias unconsciously used when drawing something much earlier in SKYBLOOD, the streets complete part of a geometric figure.

We don't show Elias rotating it.

We don't highlight it.

It simply exists.

That's for the reader who studies panels.

---

WHAT #10 DOES NOT ANSWER

We don't explain the knocking.

We don't say Elias's grandfather had powers.

We don't say he was SKYBLOOD.

We don't establish a secret society.

We don't establish Freemasonry as responsible.

We don't establish Egyptian gods as responsible.

We don't identify the figure W followed.

We don't explain why the address persisted.

We don't explain who mailed the assignment.

And we don't open whatever is knocking inside the house yet.

Because Issue #10 gives the audience an answer:

W was sent to a real address contained in the system.

Then it gives them a bigger, cleaner question:

Why was Elias's grandfather sent to the same place decades earlier?

First read:

“This thing goes back to Elias's grandfather.”

Second read:

“Doors are everywhere.”

Later reread:

“Wait—his grandfather and W were both looking up.”

That's the SKYBLOOD balance: the reader follows the story the first time and discovers the architecture underneath it later.
