END — S3 #3Analysis

This is considerably more superhero-consistent than the previous #3.

The most important page is 23. Elias finally gets an unmistakable superhero image without breaking the visual canon: lime remains PPE, navy remains workwear, silver remains reflective material, and there is zero recognition-gold. His power is communicated by mass, friction, damaged footwear and machinery changing direction.

It also gives his powers a useful hierarchy. Speed gets him to people. Strength lets him alter the physical problem. Durability lets him survive intervention—but pain and injury remain real. His judgment determines how those abilities are used. That's a much healthier long-term power set than constantly adding phenomena.

Mother's scene gives the issue its ethical question:

> “What was I supposed to do?”
“That's what I'm afraid you're gonna stop asking.”



That's important. A hero who automatically intervenes in everything can eventually become dangerous. Elias's choice remains part of the power grammar.

Malik's sequence advances the body-versus-record theme without supernatural escalation. The institution isn't evil yet. It is doing something more believable and potentially more dangerous: trying to make Malik's memory fit its data.

The unnamed man has also been reduced to the correct amount. He knows Elias's name and understands physical evidence. That's it. We have not established employer, motive, allegiance, Paper connection, supernatural knowledge, or antagonist status.

And the sabotage is now tied to ordinary operational reality: T-17 was already tagged out. Someone deliberately removed the tag and cut the brake line. That gives Elias an actionable problem.

Most importantly, the final line changes the season.

Until now Elias has largely responded.

Now:

> “Find out before they do it again.”



He's choosing to investigate because people are in danger.

That's a superhero mission generated organically by the world rather than FIRST SKY assigning him one.
