---

Analysis

This is the right escalation for #12 because it finally gives us something substantial about the adversary without explaining him.

The caller appears physically connected to 1996 and apparently hasn't aged. But we deliberately do not declare why. He could be something supernatural, a repeated identity, another body under one record, or something we haven't earned yet.

The photograph gives evidence.

It does not give doctrine.

Abel is back

This was necessary.

Asa knowing Abel predates 1996 immediately restores the Season 2 thread without dumping Abel into the room.

More importantly:

> ASA: Abel wasn't a person in the files I saw.
ELIAS: He is now.



That's a serious development.

Season 2 gave us Abel as OPEN.

Season 3 now suggests that whatever “Abel” originally represented may have acquired bodily reality.

We still don't explain it.

RJ stays disciplined

RJ's Anubis connection advances by millimeters.

He feels something through the floor.

That's it.

No earth movement.

No superhero pose.

No instant mastery.

Sister also gives us the correct family response:

> Knowing his name don't mean I know everything he does.



That prevents the Anubis connection from becoming an exposition encyclopedia.

Superhero consistency

Elias still gets a physical save: the storage manager under the shutter.

But the important choice afterward is that he doesn't solve another boundary by brute force. He thinks like a worker and finds the maintenance chase.

That's exactly the balance we need.

Scale

The entire issue weighs:

Thomas's death record / Mother's lived certainty.

Mae's recorded notification / Mae's established distrust of unsupported records.

Abel as file / Abel as person.

1996 caller / present-day caller.

And the final instruction—

> DO NOT FILE HIM TWICE.



—is essentially a warning about what happens when one apparent identity occupies two records.

Rating

9.8/10.

The only thing I would continuity-check before lock is whether the caller has been visually established enough for Elias to recognize him from a photograph. Elias has heard the caller but hasn't necessarily seen his face.

If Elias has never seen him, then Page 32 should instead have Asa recognize the photograph after Elias escapes, or we need another identification mechanism.

That's the one correction I would make before calling #12 lock-quality.

Otherwise, this keeps the superhero engine, restores Abel, advances 1996, protects RJ's progression, and gives Season 3 a real late-season acceleration without introducing a random new mythology branch.
