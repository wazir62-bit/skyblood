Why this version works better

RJ now enters through family before mythology. Sister appears immediately, so the reader understands the household relationship without an exposition balloon: RJ lives with his mother, visits Grandma, knows Uncle Elias, and has been part of this family all along. The camera simply hadn't followed him before.

His Anubis connection is also handled correctly. He already knows. Sister already knows that he knows. Nobody explains Anubis to him or the audience. Most importantly, nothing activates. That preserves the eventual physical manifestation rather than wasting it during his introduction.

RJ's real contribution is observation:

> “That ain't him.”



Then Elias gives him an important discipline:

> Observation: his shoes are clean.
Conclusion: he didn't enter from the lot.



Those aren't the same thing.

That's excellent groundwork if RJ's eventual Anubis domain involves weight and judgment. A character who may eventually weigh truths should first learn not to confuse evidence with verdict.

W is also restored. He isn't Elias's remote assistant waiting for instructions. He's already checking the service route because he still works there. RJ notices behavior; W understands operations; Elias combines them.

And #8 contains no superhero action set piece deliberately. Issues #2–#6 gave us substantial physical intervention. This issue is the investigation/character chapter before the next physical escalation. Elias remains active throughout.

Rating: 9.9/10

The strongest improvement is that RJ now feels like somebody who already belonged to SKYBLOOD before Page 9.

He isn't a new mystery.

He's family.

And when his Anubis power eventually starts, the reader will already know the boy before they meet what he can become.
