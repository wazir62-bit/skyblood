Review

This works because RJ's first power use is heroic before it is mythological.

He doesn't summon Anubis.

He doesn't transform.

Nobody attacks him.

He senses a structural failure and instinctively redirects a collapsing wall away from civilians.

That's superhero grammar:

danger → perception → choice → action → people live → consequence.

It also keeps Elias's architecture separate. Elias would probably solve that scene through physical intervention—holding, pushing, carrying.

RJ solves it by changing where the load belongs.

That's exactly the distinction we wanted.

The strongest concept

RJ says:

> “The ground told me where the weight was.”



That's the bridge between his physical and mythological domains.

Today it means:

a retaining wall.

Later it can mean:

a body.

A record.

A name.

A boundary.

Eventually:

a soul.

We don't need to say any of that yet.

One thing I would change before lock

I would probably change Sister's final:

> “I think Anubis answered him.”



It's a little too explanatory.

Because RJ already knows his Anubis connection, the family doesn't need to label the moment for the reader.

A stronger ending:

> ELIAS: What happened?

Sister looks at RJ.

SISTER:
It started.



Then RJ looks at the broken earth.

END.

That assumes history rather than explaining it.

Much stronger.

Power balance

RJ has demonstrated only two things:

Ground Sense — he perceived load/stress through physical connection.

Primitive Geokinesis — the ground responded instinctively enough to redirect a collapse.

He has not demonstrated:

True Weight, weight manipulation, Threshold Sense, Passage, advanced geokinesis, combat mastery, or anything involving death.

Keep those closed.

That gives us years of progression instead of spending Anubis in one issue.
