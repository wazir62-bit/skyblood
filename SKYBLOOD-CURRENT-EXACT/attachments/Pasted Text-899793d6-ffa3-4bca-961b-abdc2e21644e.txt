### Storm chronology + supernatural handoff — research pass

### 1. Ordinary storm first

The opening weather progression can remain entirely meteorological:

**pressure change → wind/outflow → darkening cloud → rain → lightning activity → ramp warning → freeze.**

NWS guidance supports fast-changing thunderstorm hazards including lightning, downbursts and outflow boundaries; Memphis-area NWS material specifically notes that thunderstorm downbursts can generate expanding outflow boundaries and wind shear. ([National Weather Service][1])

Nothing about that needs Elias.

In fact, the strongest presentation is that **the airport independently sees the danger after Elias has already felt it**.

Elias senses first.
Systems confirm second.
Workers obey procedure third.

That makes his perception unusual without making him the cause.

### 2. The ramp freeze is still ordinary

Once lightning reaches the operator's shutdown criteria, outdoor ramp work stopping is realistic. FAA-sponsored ACRP research specifically describes suspending exposed operations such as fueling, baggage handling and tug work during lightning hazards, while emphasizing that individual operators establish their own procedures. ([TRB Process Documentation][2])

So visually:

**warnings → GSE stops → fueling dead → bodies moving inward → apron rapidly empties.**

Then Elias breaks flow for Dez.

Nothing supernatural has happened yet.

The rescue occurs inside that ordinary storm.

This matters because the locked WB01 explicitly places **Dez's rescue before temple recognition**.

### 8. The silent strike needs to remain supernatural

This is the biggest finding in the pass.

A real nearby lightning strike is **not silent**.

NWS explains that lightning violently heats the surrounding air, creating the acoustic shock wave we call thunder. A nearby strike produces a sharp bang, crack or snap. ([National Weather Service][3])

Therefore:

**If “silent strike” means physically no thunder occurs despite a strike hitting immediately near/on Elias, that silence itself is supernatural.**

Do not try to explain it away with airport noise.

Rain, engines or distance can obscure distant thunder. They cannot credibly make a direct nearby lightning strike genuinely silent. ([National Weather Service][4])

That is useful.

It creates a clean rupture:

**ordinary lightning makes sound.
This one does not.**

The airport has already taught the reader what a thunderstorm sounds like.

Then:

**FLASH.**

No expected crack.

That absence can be more disturbing than another explosion.

### 9. The bolt itself can still resemble real lightning

The strike does not need to be a fantasy beam.

A real thunderstorm can throw lightning well outside the main rain core. NWS documents “bolts from the blue” traveling more than **25 miles** from their parent storm in some cases, and notes that lightning can emerge from the storm's anvil and strike well away from the obvious core. ([National Weather Service][5])

So visually, the strike can remain:

**white atmospheric lightning.**

Not gold.
Not a god-ray.
Not aimed from a visible Anubis hand.

What makes it impossible is the **relationship**, the silence, Elias's survival, and what follows—not necessarily its shape.
