TO BE CONTINUED.Review: 9.8/10

This is stronger than simply revealing “there are three Eliases.”

There aren't three Eliases—at least this issue doesn't establish that. The house apparition is a boundary image. That preserves the double's uniqueness while showing FIRST SKY learning from what it observes.

The key payoff from Issue #2 is immediate: we ended with Elias at Mother's door while Elias was at 2417. By Page 19, we answer the small question—there is no body on the porch—while opening the larger one: FIRST SKY can now reproduce his presentation across a boundary. That's exactly the kind of continued-story escalation that works well in serial comics: resolve the immediate cliffhanger, then intensify what it means. 

Mother wins this issue

Her best line is:

> “I received Elias Marrow before any of you knew his name.”



Importantly, she doesn't blast FIRST SKY away. She exercises the authority already established for her office: she refuses to witness its classification.

Then Paper records:

> RECEIVER DECLINES WITNESS



That's dangerous because refusal now has administrative consequence without turning Paper and FIRST SKY into the same entity.

The systems are getting cleaner

We're now developing three distinct grammars:

FIRST SKY — address, boundary, presentation, classification.

Paper — filing, status, close, surviving record.

The double — body, memory, choices, “later.”

They can interact without becoming interchangeable.

That's critical.

Page 28 is a major mythology beat

Mother asks:

> “Which one?”



And FIRST SKY hesitates.

That is more valuable than another lore explanation.

FIRST SKY is powerful.

FIRST SKY is not omniscient.

Something as large as FIRST SKY cannot presently resolve the Elias problem.

That immediately explains why both Elias and the double matter.

And then Grandfather

The last page deliberately doesn't establish that Grandfather is alive.

It establishes something more useful:

A voice behind the wall claims to be Grandfather and possesses information that destabilizes the double.

The dangerous line isn't:

> “His grandfather.”



It's:

> “Ask him why he remembers me dying.”



Because now the double's memory becomes evidence.

If he remembers Grandfather dying, where did that memory come from?

Was he there?

Did he inherit Elias's memories?

Is the memory from a different outcome?

Or—and this is the nastier possibility—is the voice lying while knowing exactly which memory will destabilize him?

Do not answer that next issue in the first five pages.

One continuity caution

I would flag Page 30's “one address” language for later review. It's good conceptually, but we should make sure “address” remains consistent with what Season 1 established rather than becoming a universal magic word.

And I would absolutely keep the scale absent again.
