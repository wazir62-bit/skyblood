END — S3 #5Analysis

This keeps the superhero architecture intact while moving the adversary forward.

The biggest development is somebody is now acting. Malik's supervisor becomes a false closure rather than the villain. The chained door establishes intentional human action, and the final page escalates that action directly into Elias's life: somebody has his workplace identity.

But I kept an important distinction: we still have not proven that the warehouse figure, T-17 sabotage, and stolen badge are the same person. Elias cannot simply declare “the villain did it.”

The scale is restored more effectively here. Malik's badge against his hospital band, then the security image with empty direct view / occupied reflection, are both genuine competing records. Neither requires someone to say “scale.”

Superhero consistency

The canopy rescue is important because Elias is off the clock and out of lime.

That proves something visually:

The vest never made him the hero.

Elias did.

His established strength and durability save the investigator. No new power appears. His existing injuries also continue accumulating, so physical action still has cost.

One continuity risk

Page 20's reflected figure needs strict handling.

It is not recognition-gold and must not be rendered as supernatural. It's ordinary reflected imagery caught by a different angle. If the artist makes it ghostly, translucent, gold, distorted, or magically impossible, we create a new power/phenomenon accidentally.

The intended fact is simpler:

The direct camera angle missed someone that the windshield angle caught.

That's detective evidence, not FIRST SKY.
