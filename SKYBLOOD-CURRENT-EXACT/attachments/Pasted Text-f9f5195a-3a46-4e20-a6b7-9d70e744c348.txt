---

Analysis

This version is much more superhero-consistent.

The previous draft gave us another vehicle attack after several machinery-heavy threats. This one changes the vocabulary completely: a human adversary physically comes for W because W possesses evidence.

That is a major season escalation.

Elias now has someone he can actually confront—but importantly, the fight does not become generic punching for spectacle. The attacker has an objective: retrieve the copy.

Elias has an objective: protect W.

Those collide.

The strongest superhero beat

Page 30–31.

The attacker escapes.

The evidence may be lost.

Elias has a clean chance to pursue.

Then W starts to fall.

Elias chooses:

> “I got you.”



That is exactly the superhero concept we've been protecting.

A superhero is not defined by catching the villain.

He's defined by who he refuses to leave behind to do it.

That also pays Mother's warning from earlier issues. Elias has not turned heroism into an impersonal job. He still evaluates the person in front of him.


---

W is substantially stronger here

W isn't waiting helplessly.

He recognizes the threat.

He lies about the copy.

He throws the first punch.

He protects the USB.

He notices the wrist band.

He survives long enough for Elias to reach him.

That keeps W a capable adult supporting character instead of turning him into a hostage.


---

The attacker finally becomes tangible

Until this point, Season 3's adversarial pressure has mostly been:

records,

credentials,

deleted footage,

calls,

sabotage,

indirect manipulation.


Now somebody puts hands on W.

That means the season has crossed a threshold.

But we still do not know:

the attacker's name,

whether he is the caller,

whether he cut T-17,

whether he chained Malik's door,

who made Elias's duplicate badge,

what organization the closed wrist band represents,

why Asa recognizes it from 1996.


We get escalation without collapsing every mystery into one villain.


---

Thomas and Mae remain controlled

The Thomas contradiction is clearer here:

officially dead → three days later an unresolved communications event occurs.

But Elias correctly refuses the lazy conclusion:

> The call does not prove Thomas called.



That protects the scale.

Likewise, Mae's name appearing as notifying relative does not automatically make Mae complicit. Mother's reaction gives us a reason to question the record without proving it false.

That's exactly the kind of two-truth tension Season 3 needs.
