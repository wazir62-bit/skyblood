Issue #12 should cash the #11 cliffhanger immediately. The reader’s rope is simple: someone is at W’s door, W recognizes them, and Elias is racing there before W opens it. This is where the threat starts feeling human without us overexplaining the mythology.

SKYBLOOD #12 — THE FACE AT THE DOOR

W is still looking through the peephole.

Three knocks.

Pause.

Three knocks.

W whispers:

“Man... what the hell you doing here?”

On the other end of the phone, Elias hears him.

“Who is it?”

W doesn't answer.

“W.”

Another knock.

W steps back from the door.

Then says:

“It's you.”

---

OPEN

Elias is already moving.

Keys.

Notebook.

Grandfather's old locker key.

Phone on speaker.

He gets in the car.

“Don't open that door.”

W:

“I ain't stupid.”

Elias:

“You was just asking why.”

“That was before you was standing outside my house.”

Elias:

“I'm in my car.”

“I know that now.”

W looks through the peephole again.

The figure is still there.

Same height.

Same build.

Same face.

Elias.

But wrong somehow.

Not distorted.

Not monstrous.

Just standing too still.

W:

“He ain't moving.”

Elias:

“Don't talk to him.”

W:

“Why?”

“I don't know.”

That answer matters.

Elias doesn't pretend he understands.

---

THE OBJECTIVE

The story is simple:

Elias has to get to W before the thing at his door gets inside.

That's Issue #12.

No archive detour.

No second investigation.

No new relic.

One line of danger.

---

THE WAIT

W stays on the phone.

The figure outside does not knock again.

That makes it worse.

W turns off the porch light.

The figure remains visible in the street glow.

Still there.

Elias drives.

Traffic.

Red light.

Construction.

For once the city itself becomes an obstacle.

No supernatural shortcut.

No wind.

No miracle.

Elias has to get there like anybody else.

W tries talking to keep himself steady.

“Your twin ugly.”

Elias:

“Good.”

“Need to lose some weight too.”

“You lying now.”

It gives the scene breath.

Then W gets quiet.

Elias hears it.

“What?”

W:

“He looking at the peephole.”

Elias:

“He was already looking at the door.”

“No.”

Pause.

“He looking at me.”

---

FIRST CHANGE

The figure raises one hand.

Slowly.

Not threatening.

Places it flat against the door.

W backs away.

No knocking.

No force.

Then the lights in W's house flicker once.

Not enough to call supernatural.

Storm residue.

Grid problem.

Anything.

W reaches for something to defend himself.

Elias tells him no.

“Don't go near the door.”

W:

“If he come through it—”

“He ain't through it yet.”

W looks again.

The porch is empty.

Figure gone.

W:

“He gone.”

Elias:

“Good.”

W:

“No. That ain't good.”

He's right.

---

THE HOUSE

A sound from the back.

Not three knocks.

A normal sound.

Fence.

Gate.

Something moving outside.

W turns.

Elias:

“Stay in the middle of the house.”

W:

“You sound like you know what you doing.”

“I don't.”

Again, honest.

W moves away from windows.

Then he notices something on the floor.

A wet footprint.

Inside.

One.

Barely visible.

W freezes.

Elias hears nothing.

“W?”

Another footprint.

Then another.

Leading from the kitchen toward the hallway.

No person.

Just wet prints.

W does not follow them.

Good.

He backs up.

Elias:

“What you seeing?”

W:

“Your ass need to drive faster.”

---

ELIAS ARRIVES

Elias pulls up hard.

W sees headlights.

Then hears Elias outside.

“I'm here.”

W doesn't open the door.

“Say something only you know.”

Elias almost laughs.

Smart.

He gives him something small and private from earlier issues.

Not mythology.

Something only W and Elias would know from work.

W unlocks the door.

Elias enters.

They look at each other.

Real Elias.

Real W.

No dramatic embrace.

W:

“You look worse than the other one.”

Elias:

“You keep talking, I'm leaving.”

Then both see the wet footprints.

Still there.

They lead down the hall.

Stop at a wall.

No door.

No vent.

Nothing.

Just wall.

W:

“Now what?”

Elias:

“Now we don't touch nothing.”

Growth.

Earlier Elias would've gone straight at it.

Now he knows better.

---

THE NOTEBOOK

Elias opens his grandfather's notebook.

Not because it magically solves the problem.

Because W saw three knocks.

Grandfather recorded three knocks.

Maybe the old man noticed something useful.

They search.

Most entries useless.

Then one line:

HEARD 3. DO NOT FOLLOW THE FAMILIAR.

Elias and W look at each other.

W:

“Could've used that about ten minutes ago.”

Below it, same date:

IT WEARS WHAT OPENS THE DOOR.

That's the first piece of language that truly scares Elias.

Not because he understands it.

Because his grandfather wrote it.

Decades earlier.

---

THE THREAT BECOMES PERSONAL

W wants to leave the house.

Reasonable.

Elias agrees.

They step outside.

Street normal.

Neighbors normal.

No figure.

They get into Elias's car.

Before pulling off, W looks back.

A shape is visible inside his front window.

Elias.

Standing in W's living room.

Both men see it.

That matters.

No ambiguity this time.

W:

“You see that?”

Elias:

“Yeah.”

For the first time, Elias has outside confirmation that something impossible is physically presenting itself in a form they both perceive.

They do not go back inside.

They leave.

---

THE CHASE

As they drive, the figure appears again.

Not running behind them.

Not teleporting dramatically.

In reflections.

Storefront glass.

A dark window.

Side mirror.

Never long enough to prove.

W sees it once.

Elias sees it twice.

Then W realizes something:

“It only show up where you reflected.”

That becomes their working theory.

Not canon truth.

Just survival logic.

Elias covers the rearview mirror.

W flips down the visor.

They stop looking at reflective surfaces.

The sightings stop.

For several pages, nothing happens.

That makes the theory feel real.

Then they pass under a dark overpass.

No mirrors.

No windows.

And the figure is standing in the road ahead.

Elias slams the brakes.

The car stops.

Figure remains.

It is Elias.

Same clothes he is wearing now.

Same expression.

Except the figure is looking at W.

Not Elias.

Then it says:

“You opened for me.”

W goes cold.

Elias hears it too.

One sentence.

That's enough.

---

THE CLIMAX

The figure steps closer.

No super-speed.

No attack.

Just walking.

Elias gets out of the car.

W grabs him.

“Man, don't.”

Elias:

“If it want me, it got me.”

W:

“It looking at me.”

Elias realizes he's right.

The figure isn't interested in Elias.

It is wearing Elias.

But it's approaching W.

Grandfather's line:

IT WEARS WHAT OPENS THE DOOR.

W didn't open the front door.

But he did recognize the face.

He spoke to it.

He engaged.

Maybe that's what “open” means.

Maybe not.

Elias doesn't know.

The figure gets closer.

Elias steps between it and W.

No power-up.

No glowing eyes.

No sky command.

Just Elias.

The figure stops.

Looks at him.

For the first time, face to face:

Elias looking at Elias.

The double tilts its head.

Then says:

“You aren't first.”

Elias:

“First what?”

No answer.

The figure moves toward W again.

Elias shoves it.

His hands make contact.

Cold.

Real.

Heavy.

The double reacts.

Not like a ghost.

Like a person.

Then wind kicks across the road.

Natural-looking.

Hard enough to throw dust.

The figure staggers one step.

Elias does not control it.

He doesn't claim it.

But the moment gives W time.

W drags Elias back.

They get in the car.

Drive.

The figure doesn't chase.

It just watches.

---

AFTERMATH

They stop somewhere public.

Bright.

Busy.

No reflective hiding places.

W is shaken.

Elias more so.

W:

“You touched it.”

“Yeah.”

“What it feel like?”

Elias thinks.

“Like me if I was dead.”

W:

“That ain't helpful.”

Elias:

“I know.”

They sit.

Then W asks the question the reader is asking:

“Why you?”

Elias:

“It wasn't after me.”

W looks at him.

That lands harder.

The thing used Elias's face to get to W.

Why?

Because W is close to Elias?

Because W followed something in #9?

Because he saw something he shouldn't?

Unknown.

One question at a time.

---

THE DISCOVERY

Elias reopens the notebook.

Searches nearby entries.

Finds another note under the same date.

Faded.

Almost missed.

DON'T ANSWER YOURSELF.

W:

“What that mean?”

Elias:

“I don't know.”

Then beneath it:

ONE OF US DID.

Now the grandfather's notes stop being atmospheric.

They imply history.

Someone in Elias's family—or someone around them—encountered this before.

But we do not explain who.

---

LAST PAGE

Elias drops W at a relative's place for the night.

He returns home alone.

His mother is awake.

He tells her only:

“W staying somewhere else tonight.”

She studies him.

“What happened?”

Elias:

“Something used my face.”

She doesn't laugh.

Doesn't dismiss him.

Instead:

“Did it speak?”

Elias freezes.

“How you know to ask that?”

Mother says nothing.

Elias steps closer.

“Ma.”

She looks at him.

Then:

“Because your grandfather used to say the worst thing wasn't seeing yourself.”

Pause.

“It was hearing what it knew.”

END.

ISSUE #12 ENGINE

WANT: Elias wants to reach W before the thing at his door gets inside.

OBSTACLE: Elias is physically far away, the figure disappears and reappears, and neither man understands what “opening the door” means.

STAKES: W may be the actual target.

THREAT: Something wearing Elias's face has reached W.

REACTION: Elias races to him.

DISCOVERY: Both men can see the same impossible figure.

ACTION: They test what seems to affect its appearances and try to get W away.

CLIMAX: Elias physically places himself between W and the double.

CONSEQUENCE: Elias learns the thing is tangible—and may not be after him at all.

TRANSFORMATION: Elias stops being merely the center of the mystery.

He becomes responsible for people endangered by proximity to him.

ONE FOREGROUND SUPERNATURAL QUESTION:

What is wearing Elias's face?

Everything points toward that.

THE HIDDEN SKYBLOOD LAYER

The surface is simple:

Double at W's door → Elias gets there → they see it together → it confronts them → they escape.

Underneath, we layer the reread material.

REFLECTION

Reflection becomes visual language, but not random confusion.

Windows.

Mirrors.

Dark glass.

Phone screens.

Water.

Metal.

The double appears near these surfaces often enough that characters form a theory.

Then we deliberately break the theory at the overpass.

That teaches the reader:

patterns can be useful without being complete.

Very SKYBLOOD.

MASONIC / ESOTERIC LAYER

The strongest hidden image is the double itself.

Not as literal Masonic doctrine.

As symbolic architecture:

self / shadow.

initiate / uninitiated.

seen / unseen.

outer man / inner man.

The road confrontation should place Elias and the double on opposite sides of subtle paired structural elements—posts, lane markings, vertical supports—so the panel reads like two figures occupying mirrored stations.

No square-and-compasses symbol.

No lecture.

EGYPTIAN LAYER

The double can visually echo the concept of a person having more than one aspect or spiritual component without us naming Ka, Ba, shadow, etc. yet.

We should not tell readers, “This is Egyptian.”

We let those echoes accumulate.

Later, one tradition may explain part of what they're seeing.

Not all of it.

GREEK LAYER

Doubles, recognition, identity and mistaken appearance can quietly echo old stories of doubles and deceptive forms.

Again:

reference, not explanation.

ILLUMINATED IMAGERY

The panel where Elias stands between W and the double should be iconic.

Road lines beneath.

Overpass structure above.

Wind and dust creating a natural border.

Three human forms:

W.

Elias.

The false Elias.

One central protective figure.

It should look like a modern illuminated page without anybody in the story noticing.

THE NUMBER LANGUAGE

Three knocks continue.

But Issue #12 introduces two Elias figures inside the same compositions.

Three and two start appearing in visual relationships.

We do not explain why.

We also deliberately include meaningless twos and threes so the comic doesn't become a Sudoku puzzle.

THE EASTER EGG

When the double first appears inside W's house, one background object is turned around compared with earlier panels of the same room.

Tiny continuity difference.

Looks like art error.

It isn't.

Much later, we'll understand that the object wasn't moved by the double.

It indicates when the room stopped being entirely ordinary.

THE GEM

Grandfather's notebook entry:

ONE OF US DID.

On first read, readers assume someone answered the double.

Later they may interpret it differently.

“Us” could mean:

family.

crew.

brothers.

people with the same condition.

members of an organization.

something else entirely.

We do not decide its final referent casually.

That line is long-range ammunition.

WHAT #12 DOES NOT ANSWER

We do not identify the double.

We do not call it a clone.

We do not call it a spirit.

We do not call it a demon.

We do not call it a doppelgänger in canon narration.

We do not explain why it uses Elias.

We do not explain why it wants W.

We do not say grandfather encountered the exact same entity.

We do not explain what “opening” means.

We do not give Elias deliberate wind control.

We do not confirm a secret society.

We do not explain Elias's mother's knowledge yet.

We answer one important thing:

W was not imagining the impossible. Elias saw it too.

Then we leave the reader with one clean larger question:

If Elias's grandfather knew about something that could wear a familiar face, who taught him what not to do?

First read:

“Something that looks like Elias came for W.”

Second read:

“The grandfather already knew rules for surviving this.”

Later reread:

“Wait—‘ONE OF US DID’ was telling us something completely different.”

That's Issue #12.
