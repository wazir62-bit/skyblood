# SKYBLOOD — Issue #1: "The Temple Above"
## Full illustrated script · 32 interior pages

*Adapted from the Episode 1 beat sheet. Canon of record is `src/lib/bible.ts`; where this script disagrees with it, the bible wins.*

**Canon-lock revision v1.2.** This is not a prose-first comic. Every page must communicate through silhouette, scale, weather, architecture, body language, and recurring visual evidence before dialogue explains anything.

---

## Cover and visual-production mandate

### Main cover

Elias stands alone on the wet Memphis cargo apron, still dressed as a worker rather than a costumed hero. His hi-vis strips catch the floodlights and form the first accidental suggestion of a heroic chest mark. Above him, the cloud pagoda occupies nearly two-thirds of the cover. The full moon sits beside its highest spire like a second eye. Elias is small, but the storm subtly bends toward him. The title **SKYBLOOD** appears in weathered gold, partly swallowed by cloud.

The cover sells a **new Black superhero at the instant before he understands what he is**. Do not show Anubis, a jackal mask, a superhero costume, or controlled lightning on Issue #1. Mystery is part of his power.

### Illustration standard

- **Cinematic density:** Every environment should feel inhabited and specific: wet steel, cargo labels, reflective tape, rivets, vapor, floodlight glare, worn kitchen surfaces, old photographs, and carved stone.
- **Readable faces:** Elias's restraint lives in micro-expression. Give reaction panels as much care as spectacle panels.
- **Hero language before costume:** Low angles, strong silhouette, reflected gold, and weather framing gradually make Elias look heroic while he remains in work clothes.
- **Weather has intention, not a face:** Clouds may compose around Elias, lean toward him, or hold impossible edges. Never draw eyes, hands, or a literal cloud creature in this issue.
- **Cultural separation:** The cloud structure uses a coherent Chinese-derived pagoda silhouette because it is repair architecture. Egyptian visual language appears only as buried geometric echoes—balance, horizon, passage, and the distant scale. Do not fuse Egyptian ornaments onto the pagoda.
- **Blackness is lived, not announced:** Hair, skin, family resemblance, home details, gesture, and workplace reality must be observed with specificity. Avoid generic faces or interchangeable features.
- **No disposable panels:** Even quiet panels must carry story evidence, emotional information, or a recurring motif.

### Color script

1. **1996:** oxidized red, chemical white, bruised midnight blue.
2. **Work and home:** sodium amber, industrial green, rain-black steel, warm brown skin, lived-in kitchen warmth.
3. **The unmoving cloud:** progressively drain local color while preserving Elias's skin and gold reflections.
4. **The temple reveal:** moon-silver cloud, charcoal depth, restrained ancient gold only where light catches edges.
5. **After the strike:** near-monochrome blue-black; human warmth returns only when Elias reaches home.
6. **The tower:** mineral black, cold grey stone, water sheen, one distant gold line on the unmoving scale.

### Recurring visual grammar

- **Circles:** moon, coffee rim, radar return, condensation, eye, camera lens, water basin. Recognition gathering around a center.
- **Lines and gaps:** shift-board rows, runway markings, pagoda tiers, missing person in the photograph, blank stone band. Record versus erasure.
- **Hands:** grandmother holding a camera, supervisor removing a name, Elias correcting the board, sister receiving the phone, Keeper washing names. A hand either maintains a record or lets it disappear.
- **Gold:** never a general filter. Use it only when the sky recognizes Elias or when an ancient office is faintly present.
- **The accidental emblem:** Elias's reflective vest creates a split-wing/scale silhouette in key panels. This may later inform his superhero symbol, but nobody notices it yet.

### Splash-page rule

Reserve true visual excess for pages 4, 20–21, 24, and 32. Each must reveal a different scale: personal absence, celestial architecture, bodily survival, and mythic machinery. The reader should feel the universe widening four times.

## Letterer's note — read before page 1

This issue runs on a **sound effect vocabulary that gets taken away.**

Pages 1–22 are loud. Ambient SFX are constant, small, and unglamorous: ramp noise, belt loaders, rain on metal, the shift board clacking. Letter them plainly, in the background layer, never as splash lettering. The reader should stop noticing them by page 10. That is the point.

**The lightning strike on page 23 produces no thunder. From that strike through the end of the issue, there are no ambient SFX. None. Not one.** No wind, no rain, no machinery, no footsteps. Dialogue balloons continue. Everything else is silent.

Nobody in the story remarks on it. The reader feels it before they can name it, which is the whole book in one craft decision — and it is also exactly what the Unweighed does to a person's record.

## ACT ONE — The Man Who Watches the Sky

### PAGE FIVE (5 panels)

**5.1** — Wide establishing. **MEMPHIS.** The cargo hub at 11:40pm, seen across the field: apron floodlights, aircraft nose-on, belt loaders, tugs, the long low sort building. Everything wet. Sodium amber on standing water.

> CAPTION: Memphis. Now. 11:40 PM.

SFX: *WHRRRRRR* / *chk-chk-chk-chk* / *beep beep beep*

**5.2** — Inside the sort. Cans, cages, rollers, hi-vis. **ELIAS MARROW, 35 — played by Ceril**, moves through it with the practiced economy of a man who recognizes trouble before anyone else sees it. He is mature, solidly built, close-cropped, and observant; preserve Ceril's recognizable likeness without aging Elias beyond 35 or redesigning him between panels.

**5.3** — A younger worker, **DEZ**, wrestles a mis-scanned can. Elias reaches past him, turns the label, and it scans. He doesn't make a thing of it.

> DEZ: How do you always —
> ELIAS: Label's upside down. Every time.

**5.4** — Elias straightens. Stops. Looks up — through the open bay door, at a sky we can't see from this angle.

**5.5** — Close on his face. Nothing mystical. He's *listening* with his skin.

> ELIAS: Hold's coming.

> **Artist:** This is Elias's first hero portrait. The floodlight behind him should create a thin gold edge along his cheek and shoulder while the crossed reflective bands on his vest suggest a balanced scale. Keep it natural enough that readers discover the emblem rather than being shown it.

**6.2** — Time cut. Same bay. The hold klaxon lights. Everyone is already moving; nobody looks surprised.

Break room. Vending machines, one working microwave, a taped-up schedule.

**7.2** —

### PAGE EIGHTEEN (4 panels)

**18.1** — Elias turns back into the weather. He braces against the moving net, frees Dez's boot, and shoves him toward shelter. Elias takes the dangerous side without hesitation.

**18.2** — Dez runs for the building and looks back. Elias is now alone, halfway between the aircraft and the bay. His decision put him there. He should be following. He isn't.

**18.3** — From behind: the last bay door coming down. Yellow light narrowing to a strip. He does not turn around.

**18.4** — Close. No defiance in it. He feels a directional pressure in the air — a wrongness that seems to hold on him. He has not seen the structure yet, and nothing has confirmed that he is being watched. He should be going inside. Instead, he lifts his head.

### PAGES TWENTY & TWENTY-ONE (double-page spread — 1 panel)

**20–21.1** — **THE TEMPLE ABOVE.**

A multi-tiered pagoda, colossal, occupying the entire sky over the cargo hub. It is made of cloud and it is unmistakably *architecture* — tiers, eaves, columns, a spire. Moonlight catches the upper roofs like wet stone. The lower tiers dissolve into the storm.

The architecture must be richly illustrated and structurally believable: repeated bracket sets, roof ridges, column bays, deep interior darkness, and cloud behaving like carved mass. It should reward a reader who studies the spread for a full minute. Hide three discoveries in the structure: one unreadable band where names should be, one doorway whose proportions echo a human silhouette, and one lower tier subtly out of alignment as if the repair is bearing too much weight.

Elias is a hi-vis speck on wet concrete at the very bottom of the spread. The hub buildings are a strip of small warm lights beneath a structure that dwarfs the entire airfield.

No caption. No dialogue. No SFX.

> **Artist:** Hold this. The reader should have time to search the clouds themselves. This is the image the whole book exists to deliver — everything before it is setup, everything after it is consequence.

### PAGE TWENTY-FOUR (1 panel — full page)

**24.1** — Elias flat on his back on the wet cargo apron. Camera high overhead, looking almost straight down. Rain is still falling — we can see it — and it makes no sound. The floodlights are on beyond the composition. The world is exactly as loud as it was.

His body, the silver reflective bands on his soaked vest, existing apron stand/safety markings, drainage seams, and standing water fall into an accidental heroic geometry from this overhead viewpoint. No literal symbol is drawn and no marking is designed around him. Recognition-light catches the existing reflective media — vest bands, wet paint, water, and small metal details — against the drained blue-black page. This is the first unmistakably iconic image of the future superhero, created entirely from his existing world.

And there is nothing on the page.

No SFX. No caption. No dialogue.

> **Letterer:** This page is the hinge of the issue. Nothing but art. From here to the end of the book, no ambient sound effect appears again.

### PAGE THIRTY-TWO (4 panels)

**32.1** — Hallway. A closed bedroom door. Elias in front of it, phone in his hand, coat still on.

**32.2** — Close on his hand, raised, not yet knocking.

**32.3** — He knocks.

**32.4** — Interior. **STONE.** Immense, cold, curving away.

An OLD MAN in worn layers works along a wall of carved names with a shallow basin of water and a wide brush. Wet the stone, read, move on. His pace is that of a man on a schedule he cannot meet.

He passes a band of stone that is completely smooth. No marks. Nothing to read. He does not stop. He has passed it a thousand times.

Far below him in the dark, half out of frame, something like a **scale.**

Perfectly still.

The final panel should occupy at least two-thirds of the page. Give the carved wall thousands of individually suggested marks so its maintenance feels impossible. The Keeper is tiny but legible. The smooth band cuts across the density like a wound. The scale is not decorative Egyptian iconography; it is distant machinery whose symmetry answers the accidental emblem formed by Elias's vest on pages 5 and 24.

### Character-consistency lock

Every final panel must be checked against the approved character sheet. Do not change Elias's face, complexion, hairline, build, age, uniform construction, or reflective-strip geometry between pages. Weather and lighting may transform how he reads; they may not redesign him. The same rule applies to every recurring character and location.

### Issue #1 superhero promise

The reader must finish this issue believing three things at once: Elias is an ordinary Black working man; the sky has recognized him as something ancient; and the heroic figure he may become is already visible in the way the world frames him. Issue #1 reveals the **silhouette of a superhero**, not the finished costume or full power set.
