Absolutely. I'm stripping #9 down to a clean thriller spine: W is missing, Elias needs to find him before something happens to him. The mythology stays beneath the floorboards.

SKYBLOOD #9 — THE OTHER SIDE OF DAY

W doesn't show up for his new shift.

At first, nobody thinks that's strange.

People miss work.

Except W didn't call.

And W always calls.

OPEN

Several days have passed since W was moved from nights.

Elias is adjusting to working without him.

The new arrangement has already become routine enough to be irritating.

Then, near the end of Elias's shift, he asks someone coming in from days:

“How W liking daylight?”

The answer:

“W ain't been here.”

Elias thinks he means today.

“No. Since they moved him.”

That lands.

W was removed from Elias's crew for safety.

But W apparently never reported to the crew he was moved to.

Elias calls him.

Straight to voicemail.

Again.

Voicemail.

Elias tells himself not to turn everything into something.

Maybe W got mad.

Maybe he quit.

Maybe he took time off.

Then Elias remembers their last ordinary conversation.

W never said he was leaving.

And W left something in his old locker.

Something he would've taken if he planned not to return.

Nothing mystical.

His work gloves.

Elias looks at them.

Now he's worried.

---

THE OBJECTIVE

The story becomes extremely simple:

Find W.

Elias asks the supervisor.

W was reassigned.

That's all the supervisor knows.

Elias asks where.

Days.

“What department?”

The supervisor checks.

Stops.

Checks again.

W's name isn't on the day roster.

That is the first real problem.

The supervisor isn't sinister.

He's confused too.

“Probably ain't updated.”

Elias:

“Then where they send him?”

Nobody immediately knows.

The paperwork that separated W from Elias exists.

The paperwork showing where W went does not.

Elias leaves work and starts looking.

---

THE SEARCH

Elias goes to W's place.

Truck gone.

No answer.

He calls again.

Nothing.

He checks somewhere W regularly stops after work.

Nothing.

The search moves through the city.

This gives SKYBLOOD something important:

the world outside the yard.

Memphis becomes part of the comic rather than a name printed beneath establishing shots.

Elias drives roads W would reasonably use.

He asks ordinary people ordinary questions.

No mystic guide appears.

No ancient stranger gives him directions.

This is Elias looking for his friend.

But underneath the search, the hidden SKYBLOOD machinery is running.

Readers may notice repeated architecture.

Three streetlights followed by one dark pole.

A building whose window arrangement resembles geometry seen much earlier.

An old stone detail embedded in newer construction.

A faded emblem whose pieces could be interpreted several different ways.

Elias notices none of it.

He is looking for W.

---

THE FIRST BREAK

At a gas station, Elias gets something useful.

The clerk recognizes W's truck.

W was there recently.

Elias shows a picture.

“Yeah. Him.”

“When?”

The clerk gives a time.

Elias stops.

That time matters.

W was at this gas station during the period when Elias believed W was already working days.

So W wasn't simply staying home.

He was going somewhere.

The clerk remembers one more thing.

W asked for directions.

“To where?”

The clerk doesn't remember the name.

But he remembers W showing him an address.

Elias asks if the cameras caught it.

Maybe.

Now we have a grounded investigative sequence rather than another supernatural revelation.

They find the footage.

W enters.

Buys something.

Shows his phone.

Talks.

Leaves.

Nothing strange.

Then Elias notices W looking outside.

The clerk doesn't remember that part.

On the footage, W stares through the glass for several seconds.

Elias asks:

“What was he looking at?”

They rewind.

Different camera.

Outside.

Cars.

Pumps.

People.

Nothing obvious.

But W is clearly looking at something.

Then he walks outside.

Not toward his truck.

Across the lot.

Camera loses him.

Thirty seconds later W returns.

Gets into his truck.

Leaves.

Elias:

“Where'd he go?”

Clerk:

“I don't know.”

That is our supernatural question for #9.

Only one:

What did W see?

---

THE ROAD

The clerk remembers enough of the directions to give Elias a general area.

Elias searches.

Eventually he finds W's truck.

Parked beside an access road.

Empty.

Door locked.

No obvious struggle.

Elias calls W's phone.

This time—

he hears it.

Faintly.

Somewhere nearby.

Elias follows the sound.

The phone is lying beside the road.

Screen cracked.

No W.

Now the story has moved from concern to danger.

Elias calls the police.

That matters.

He behaves like a real person.

While explaining where he is, Elias notices footprints leaving the roadside.

He doesn't wait.

He follows.

---

ELIAS'S PROBLEM

The trail enters an industrial area.

Old structures mixed with newer construction.

Fences.

Drainage.

Concrete.

Service passages.

Nothing that announces:

SECRET MYTHOLOGY HERE.

Elias keeps calling W's name.

Then the weather changes.

Subtle.

Pressure.

Wind direction.

That familiar sensation.

The same awareness that has complicated Elias's life.

He stops.

After what happened in #8, he doesn't trust himself.

His sensing got W moved.

Now W is missing.

For a moment Elias considers ignoring it.

Then he realizes:

Whatever his perception is—

W believed him before anybody else did.

Elias listens.

Not magically.

Carefully.

Wind.

Metal.

Traffic.

Water.

Then underneath it—

a sound.

Three knocks.

Pause.

Three knocks.

Elias follows.

---

FINDING W

W is trapped below grade inside an old service structure.

Alive.

Injured.

Angry.

Very happy to see Elias without admitting it.

Elias:

“What the hell you doing down here?”

W:

“Currently? Waiting on your slow ass.”

That releases the tension without destroying it.

Elias calls emergency services again and gives the updated location.

W's leg is pinned.

Elias can't simply pull him free.

Part of the structure is unstable.

Now we have the climax:

Elias found W. Now he has to keep him alive.

---

WHAT HAPPENED?

While they wait, Elias asks the obvious question.

“How you get down here?”

W doesn't have a good answer.

He remembers the gas station.

He remembers driving.

He remembers seeing something beside the road.

“What?”

W struggles.

Not mystical amnesia.

He genuinely cannot describe it.

“A person?”

“I don't know.”

“Animal?”

“No.”

“Then what?”

W gets irritated.

“I said I don't know.”

Then:

“It looked familiar.”

That's all we give the reader.

Elias asks familiar how.

W:

“Like I was supposed to know where it was going.”

That's enough.

No mythology lecture.

Back to survival.

---

THE CLIMAX

The structure shifts.

They don't have time to wait.

Water begins entering through a damaged drainage channel.

Not a supernatural flood.

A real physical danger.

W is pinned.

Elias studies the debris.

Years around freight, equipment and loads matter.

He understands weight.

He understands what happens when one thing shifts and transfers force somewhere else.

This is not a new superpower.

This is Elias Marrow's life before SKYBLOOD becoming useful to SKYBLOOD.

He finds a way to relieve enough pressure to move W.

But doing it puts Elias in the dangerous position.

W realizes it.

“No.”

Elias:

“What?”

“You know what.”

Elias keeps working.

W:

“Don't trade places with me.”

Elias:

“Ain't nobody trading nothing.”

He moves the support.

Weight shifts.

W pulls free.

Then the temporary support fails.

Elias is trapped on the wrong side.

W could leave.

He doesn't.

Issue #1 echoes.

Again, W moves toward Elias.

Together they get out as emergency crews arrive.

No wind saves them.

No lightning.

No transformation.

Two men survive because neither leaves the other.

---

AFTERMATH

W is treated.

Nothing life-threatening.

Elias sits nearby.

W finally asks:

“How you found me?”

“Your phone.”

“No. Down there.”

Elias tells him about the knocking.

W looks confused.

“I wasn't knocking.”

Elias thinks W is joking.

W isn't.

“You ain't hit nothing?”

“No.”

Three knocks.

Pause.

Three knocks.

Elias doesn't tell him exactly what he heard.

Not yet.

Then W remembers something.

Before the fall, he took a picture.

He doesn't remember why.

His phone is damaged, but the image synced.

They retrieve it.

The photograph looks almost useless.

Road.

Fence.

Part of an old structure.

Sky.

W:

“See? I don't even know why I took that.”

Elias studies it.

There is no monster.

No ghost.

No second Elias.

Nothing obvious.

Then Elias notices something in the distance.

A person.

Very small.

Standing near the edge of the frame.

Too far away to identify.

Elias zooms.

Pixelation.

Still impossible.

W:

“That who I followed?”

Elias:

“I don't know.”

And that's where we stop the explanation.

---

LAST PAGE

Elias goes home.

He keeps thinking about the three knocks.

He looks at W's photograph again.

The distant figure remains meaningless.

But the reader gets something Elias doesn't.

Behind the figure is an old wall.

Its surface contains ordinary repairs, seams and discoloration.

Together they form part of a geometric arrangement we've quietly been building across earlier issues.

No caption identifies it.

No zoom.

No explanation.

Then we cut back to the gas-station security footage.

The panel we saw earlier.

W staring outside.

This time the printed comic gives us slightly more of the frame.

W isn't looking at the pumps.

He isn't looking toward where the mysterious figure later appears.

He's looking upward.

At the reflection of the sky in the glass.

And inside that reflection—

something interrupts the clouds.

Not enough to identify.

Maybe architecture.

Maybe shadow.

Maybe nothing.

END.

ISSUE #9 ENGINE

WANT: Elias wants to find W.

OBSTACLE: Nobody can tell Elias where W was actually sent, and W has disappeared.

STAKES: W may be injured or in immediate danger.

That's the entire surface story.

A reader never has to decode anything else to understand #9.

THREAT: W is missing.

REACTION: Elias starts looking.

DISCOVERY: W appears to have followed something he cannot describe.

ACTION: Elias tracks him to the service structure.

CLIMAX: Elias must free W before the structure fails and water traps them.

CONSEQUENCE: W survives.

TRANSFORMATION: Elias learns that whatever is happening around him may now be affecting people who choose to stand close to him.

ONE FOREGROUND SUPERNATURAL QUESTION:

What did W see?

We do not add another one.

THE HIDDEN SKYBLOOD LAYER

This is where the complexity lives without confusing the story.

Three knocks recur.

Three lights / one dark.

Road intersections quietly create geometry.

Certain architecture contains Egyptian, Greek and Masonic visual echoes without being labeled.

One composition subtly resembles an illuminated manuscript: Elias and W occupy the central action while machinery, beams and cables create the “border.”

The direction W traveled can later prove significant.

The gas-station time can later prove significant.

The position of the sun can later prove significant.

The old wall can later prove significant.

Even the exact place where W was pinned can eventually matter.

But none of that is required to understand:

Elias's friend disappeared. Elias went looking for him. Elias found him. They got each other out.

That's SKYBLOOD's new balance.

THE GEM

There should be one extremely difficult Easter egg.

Somewhere during Elias's drive, he passes an ordinary directional sign.

Readers won't care about it.

Much later in SKYBLOOD, when we finally understand part of the ancient hidden system, that sign's arrows, distances and road numbers will reproduce a relationship we've been showing since Issue #1.

We never announce the callback.

Readers discover it.

FINAL EFFECT

First read:

“Damn. W disappeared and Elias went after him.”

Second read:

“Wait. Something led W there.”

Later-season reread:

“Hold up—the route, the wall, the three knocks, the sky reflection... this was already part of it.”

The reader knows exactly what happened.

They simply don't know yet what it means.

That is the difference between confusion and mystery.This is the #9 I'd put after The Other Road. It continues #8 directly, strengthens Elias/W, gives us danger and a rescue, expands Memphis, and still leaves enough buried underneath for SKYBLOOD readers to tear apart years later.
