TO BE CONTINUED.Revised rating: 9.6 / 10

This pass materially improves the issue because the mystery is now coming from meaning, not from uncertain staging.

The biggest improvement is Pages 17–18. Previously, “I wrote this at work” required the reader to infer what “this” referred to. Now the comic establishes a clean visual chain: old tape → surviving M → Elias recognizes it → work notebook → repeated unconscious M's. That also adds a disturbing implication: Mae's name was already beginning to surface through Elias before his mother spoke it.

Page 25 is stronger because the “thousand passes” concept is now something an artist can actually stage. The unnamed man doesn't need exposition; repetition tells us he is ancient, ritualized, trapped, assigned, or persistent. Most importantly, Mae's name makes him break pattern.

Page 26 now becomes more threatening too. The distant watcher reacts metaphysically; the person across the street records the event bureaucratically. Those are two completely different threat systems noticing the same house.

Page 31 is clearer without giving away the stone. The reader needs to recognize, “I've seen that object before.” They do not need to understand what the emerging letter means yet. That's the correct form of comic-book mystery.

I also think Page 19 became stronger with:

> MOTHER: Her.



That tiny answer grounds “find it again.” The next line—

> Names are how the sky keeps a body.



—can then carry the mythology without confusing the literal action.

The strongest stretch is now 17–26. That sequence does a tremendous amount of work: inherited evidence, unconscious transmission, choice, naming, reception, temporary visual proof, Mother's true role, distant reaction, and local surveillance. Yet it still feels like one escalating family scene rather than a lore dump.

One thing I would not change: Elias's ending.

> “A cargo worker. With a week left of paid shock.”



Keep it. The cosmic mythology is expanding rapidly, and Elias continuing to describe himself in ordinary working-class terms prevents SKYBLOOD from becoming self-important.

At 9.6, I would consider the story architecture essentially locked. The remaining gains are mostly going to come from page composition, panel rhythm, expressions, silence, and exactly how much visual information the artist reveals on Pages 21–26 and 31–32, not from adding more plot.
