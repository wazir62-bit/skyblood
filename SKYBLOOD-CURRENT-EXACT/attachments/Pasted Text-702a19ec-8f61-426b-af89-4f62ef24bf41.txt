I would not activate RJ's earth powers yet. #8 just introduced him properly. Let #10 belong primarily to Elias while bringing Abel back into the active season, because we've gone long enough without paying that Season 2 continuity.

Analysis

This issue gets Season 3 back to one of its most important promises:

> Elias was never the event. He is evidence that an event already happened.



And now we have our first concrete historical fracture:

Thomas was officially dead before the 1996 payphone event.

That does not tell us Thomas made the call.

It does not resurrect him.

It doesn't explain FIRST SKY.

It gives us two pieces of evidence that cannot presently coexist cleanly.

That's the scale.

Asa finally earns his presence

Instead of inventing a new antagonist name, I used Asa, because Asa was one of the Season 2 threads you specifically said Season 3 couldn't park.

But notice what we still don't know:

Who employs Asa?
What exactly does he investigate?
Why did the caller know him?
What happened in 1996?
Whether Asa is trustworthy.

We only learn enough to justify why he's been following Elias.

RJ progression

RJ advances one notch, not ten.

He does not move earth.

He doesn't invoke Anubis.

He doesn't control weight.

He experiences what we can later recognize as primitive Ground Sense:

> “Floor heavy.”



Then he does the most heroic thing available with the ability he currently possesses:

he warns people.

That's important. Powers don't become heroic because they look spectacular. The choice does.

His first true geokinetic manifestation can therefore still have weight later.

Elias remains the superhero

Elias gets the major physical intervention.

He catches the failing porch structure while Asa shuts off the gas.

It's cooperative—which is healthier than Elias solving every emergency alone.

And it avoids another door.

Rating

9.8/10.

My one revision concern is the final Thomas document. It's powerful enough that we should continuity-check the exact established Thomas dates before locking the “three days” interval. The contradiction should be precise, not convenient.

Otherwise, #10 does what a midseason convergence issue should: it makes earlier seasons more important while continuing the superhero story happening now.
