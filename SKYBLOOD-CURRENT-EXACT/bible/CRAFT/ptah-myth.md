## The myth, short

Ptah is the craftsman of Memphis.

He does not birth the world from his body the way Atum does (semen, spit, fingers). He **thinks** it in the heart (*ib*) and **speaks** it with the tongue. Every thing gets a name. Horus takes shape in the heart; Thoth in the tongue. Heart is in every body; tongue in every mouth. That is the Shabaka theology.

He is patron of **makers** — sculptors, metalworkers, the people who put form into matter. Statues live because a mouth is opened for them (Opening of the Mouth: adze, touch, breath). A figure without that rite is wood. A figure with it can receive offerings.

His house-name is **Hut-ka-Ptah** — enclosure of the *ka* of Ptah. Greeks heard that as *Aigyptos*. The country’s later name is, in one etymology, **Ptah’s ka-compound**.

Wife Sekhmet (power, plague, healing). Son Nefertem (lotus, first light). Later glue: Ptah-Sokar-Osiris. Do not cast the family as that triad.

Apis, the bull, is sometimes called a living *ba* of Ptah. Do not put a bull on the apron.

## Opening of the Mouth vs the stamp

Opening of the Mouth gives a statue **the right to have a mouth** — to eat, to speak, to be a person in the offering cult.

Their stamp does the opposite. It gives a **file** the right to speak *for* a person.

6-26:

> They can stamp a living name.  
> They cannot stamp a mouth.

That line is Ptah’s myth in cargo English. The rite they do not have. The office they counterfeit.

If a later book ever needs a visual rhyme: an ordinary tool touching a face (safety, first aid, a mother wiping a plate) — not an adze, not a priest. The kitchen already does this. She washes a plate. That is also a record.
