## Promise (put this at the top of the writers’ bible)

> SKYBLOOD follows an ordinary man discovering that the impossible things surrounding his life were never random. Every answer transforms Elias Marrow while exposing a larger mystery. His enemies do not merely threaten his life — they challenge his understanding of his blood, his power, his family, and the hidden structure of the world around him. The reader should always understand what Elias wants and what threatens him, while never being completely certain what SKYBLOOD ultimately means.

> We are not watching a superhero’s adventures. We are witnessing the events that make a man become one.

## What the comic must be

SKYBLOOD is a superhero **being born**, not an established hero having adventures.

Elias does not begin understanding who he is, what he can do, why the sky answers feeling, what Mae’s erasure means, or what has noticed him.

The audience discovers **alongside him**.

Discovery alone is not enough. **Every stage of change happens because something threatens** him, someone he loves, his city, his understanding, or something larger.

Opposition need not be costumed. It can be a person, an organization, an ancient intelligence, something released, something hunting, **or a truth**.

It can never become Elias wandering mythology and finding out he is special.

**Mythology creates questions. Danger forces answers.**

---

## The engine (every major arc)

**MYSTERY → THREAT → REACTION → DISCOVERY → ACTION → CONSEQUENCE → CLIMAX → TRANSFORMATION → DEEPER MYSTERY**

1. He sees / feels something he cannot explain.  
2. Something begins happening because of it.  
3. He reacts as the **ordinary man he currently is** (vest, clock-in, look away).  
4. That reaction reveals another piece.  
5. Knowledge forces a **decision**.  
6. The decision has a cost.  
7. Confrontation.  
8. He is different — not necessarily stronger.  
9. The mystery was larger.

Then the cycle repeats.

## Backstory is ammunition

Nothing in the past exists only to make him interesting.

Childhood, family, Memphis, work, storms, things adults said, inherited objects, places, dreams, superstitions, coincidences he dismissed — **may** become evidence later.

**Resist making everything supernatural.** Sometimes a memory is a memory. That is what makes a real connection land (“they showed us that in Issue #2”).

Clues must **exist before the answer.** EG01 Study gems are the on-page instrument of this rule.

See `LOCKED/SB-BS01-backstory-inventory.md` for what is already loaded vs still blank.

---

## Hidden knowledge is machinery, not wallpaper

Egyptian, funerary, name-lore, cargo procedure, numbers, architecture, astronomy — only if they **do a job**.

- A building’s geometry might matter (apron paint + H-wrap on Page 24).  
- A letter might recur (**M—**).  
- Myth may be corrupted history or a fragment.  
- Ritual may be technology later cultures misunderstood.  
- We may let the audience believe one explanation, then break it.

**We do not tell the audience which mythology is “right.”**

Civilizations may have **inherited pieces**, not invented the source. That is a storytelling possibility — **not** a historical claim locked as plot.

Published visual law still stands: pagoda = repair; Egyptian = buried offices; Anubis = angle in #1; no jackal-in-frame.

---

## Power development is character development

No traditional power inventory.

Issue #1 restrictions remain the **floor** until a named sheet lifts a specific one: no deliberate weather control, lightning, flight, passage, guardian form, designed emblem, knowing Anubis talk.

Every new ability requires at least one of: **Discovery. Understanding. Cost. Choice.** Often all four.

Greatest evolution is not “a bigger storm.” It is **understanding what the storm has been telling him** — mastery through listening and restraint.

#4 kept no-command as a **decision**, not a level-up. Do not “unlock weather” because the ban calendar expired.

---

## Villains attack the mythology

The best antagonist holds a **competing piece of the truth**.

Not locked plots — pressure examples:

- He thinks the bloodline protects. They think it is a **key**.  
- He thinks the temple came for him. They ask if it came **to use** him.  
- He keeps a record. They **file** it.

The contractor already does this (counterfeit Weigh). Future threats should argue **interpretation**, not only punch.

---

## Anubis must remain dangerous to understand

Do not resolve: benevolent / manipulative / both / wrong question.

Ancestor-gives-powers is **too easy** and **not locked**.

Love and use can coexist. Protection can have a price. The audience may be asking the wrong question.

Until the owner opens ancestry or speech, Anubis stays: **camera, silence, title, scale.** Not a mentor.

---

## Revelation levels (not locked seasons)

Useful architecture only:

1. Something is happening to me. *(#1)*  
2. Something has been happening to my family. *(#2–4: Mae, Mother, Sister)*  
3. Something happened long before my family.  
4. People have known about this. *(contractor is the first crack)*  
5. What everyone calls mythology may be pieces of something larger.

Every level still needs a **concrete threat**, human stakes, a decision, a climax. Do not sacrifice the comic for the iceberg.

Surface: a damn good superhero comic.  
Under: mystery.  
Under that: mythology.  
Under that: hidden history.  
Under that: the truth — **revealed last, if ever.**
